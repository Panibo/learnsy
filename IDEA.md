# Learnsy Missions

## Concept

**Learn one thing. Do one thing.**

Learnsy Missions turns sustainability learning into a personalized cycle of:

**Understand → Learn → Act → Reflect → Continue**

Instead of giving employees a catalogue of sustainability courses, Learnsy helps each person understand:

- Why a sustainability topic matters to their role
- What they should learn next
- What small action they can take at work
- How that action should influence their next learning step

The aim is to make learning feel less like training that has been assigned and more like a journey that evolves around the employee.

---

# 1. The Problem

Learnsy already identifies an important gap.

There is plenty of sustainability and ESG learning available, but employees may struggle to understand which content is relevant to them or how company sustainability goals connect to their own role.

A sustainability manager may understand the broader goals, while someone working in sales, IT, HR, finance, or operations may not immediately understand:

**“What does this have to do with me?”**

Learnsy Missions addresses that gap.

Rather than starting with content, the experience starts with the employee’s context.

---

# 2. Core Value Proposition

### For employees

**“Show me what sustainability means for my job, teach me one useful thing, and give me something realistic I can do with it.”**

### For companies

Learnsy Missions helps translate high-level sustainability objectives into:

**Company goal → employee relevance → learning → action**

Instead of measuring only whether employees completed training, the platform can begin exploring whether learning is actually being applied.

---

# 3. The Experience

## Step 1 — Understand the Employee

When someone first enters Learnsy, the system builds a lightweight learning profile.

It could consider:

**Role**
Sales Manager

**Department**
Sales

**Experience**
5 years

**Interests**
Customer relationships, climate strategy

**Existing knowledge**
Basic sustainability knowledge

**Company goals**
Reduce Scope 3 emissions
Improve sustainability reporting

The employee should not have to complete a long questionnaire.

The platform could ask a few simple questions and progressively learn from their behavior.

For example:

> What would you most like to understand better?

- How sustainability affects my customers
- How sustainability affects my daily work
- Our company's sustainability goals
- Sustainability regulations
- I'm not sure yet

Instead of presenting a content library first, Learnsy presents a personalized mission.

For example:

## Your Mission

**Why this matters to you**

The employee immediately understands:

**Why me?**

**Why now?**

**What will I learn?**

**What can I do with it?**

# 5. The Mission Structure

Every mission has four main stages.

## 1. Why

Explain why the topic matters to the individual.

The explanation should reference the employee's:

- Role
- Company priorities
- Previous learning
- Interests
- Existing knowledge

Example:

> You're working in procurement, where supplier decisions can strongly affect your company's environmental footprint. Understanding Scope 3 emissions can help you identify which supplier questions matter most.

This is where AI can create the strongest feeling of personalization.

---

## 2. Learn

Recommend one small, relevant learning resource.

This could be:

- A short video
- Article
- Course section
- Webinar
- Internal company material
- External learning resource
- AI-generated explanation

The important design principle is:

### Do not overwhelm the employee with choices.

Instead of:

> Here are 37 courses about carbon emissions.

Learnsy says:

> Based on your role and goals, this is the best place to start.

The employee can still explore alternatives if they want.

---

## 3. Act

Turn learning into a small workplace action.

The mission should be achievable within normal work.

Examples:

### Sales

**Learning**
Understanding green claims

**Action**
Review one sustainability claim in your current sales material and identify what evidence supports it.

---

### HR

**Learning**
Sustainability culture

**Action**
Look at your current onboarding process and identify one place where your company's sustainability goals could be explained more clearly.

---

### Procurement

**Learning**
Supplier sustainability

**Action**
Choose one supplier and identify one sustainability question you could add to your next discussion with them.

---

### IT

**Learning**
Digital sustainability

**Action**
Identify one part of your team's technology usage where energy consumption could potentially be reduced.

---

### Finance

**Learning**
ESG metrics

**Action**
Choose one sustainability metric and identify where it could affect a financial or investment decision.

The action should be intentionally small.

The objective is not:

> Save the planet today.

It is:

> Apply one thing you just learned.

---

# 6. Reflection

After completing the action, Learnsy asks a short reflection.

For example:

### What happened?

**Did you complete the mission?**

Yes
Partially
Not yet

Then:

### What did you discover?

The employee can type a short response or choose from options.

For example:

> Our customers care more about supplier emissions data than I expected.

or:

> I couldn't find the information I needed.

The reflection becomes useful input for the next recommendation.

---

# 7. Adaptive Next Mission

This is where the experience becomes a journey rather than a sequence of courses.

Instead of automatically moving the employee to “Lesson 2,” Learnsy considers what happened.

Example:

Employee says:

> Customers keep asking us about product carbon footprints.

Learnsy could respond:

### Based on what you discovered, you might want to explore:

**How product carbon footprints are calculated**

Why this is relevant:

> Your customer conversations suggest that product-level emissions information is becoming important in your sales process.

The learning journey therefore responds to real work.

---

# 8. Curiosity-Based Branching

Employees should have some control over where they go next.

After completing a mission:

### Where do you want to go next?

**Understand the bigger picture**

How Scope 3 fits into corporate climate strategy

**Apply it to customers**

How to discuss emissions with customers

**Go deeper**

Understanding Scope 3 calculation methods

**Try another action**

Apply what you learned somewhere else in your work

This makes the learning experience exploratory rather than completely predetermined.

---

# 9. Mission Path

Over time, employees could see their learning journey visually.

For example:

## Your sustainability journey

**Company climate goals**
↓
✓ Why Scope 3 matters

↓

✓ Scope 3 for Sales

↓

✓ Customer sustainability conversation

↓

**Current**
Product carbon footprints

↓

Locked / suggested:
Communicating climate claims responsibly

The important psychological difference is that this is not presented as:

**32% of mandatory course completed**

Instead, the employee sees a story of what they have explored and applied.

# 10. AI's Role

AI should not simply exist as a chatbot.

Its role should be specific and useful.

AI can help with:

### Personalization

Determine which topics are most relevant based on:

Role + goals + interests + knowledge + previous activity

---

### Relevance explanations

Explain:

**Why should I learn this?**

Example:

> Because you work in product design, circular economy principles could influence decisions you make about materials, durability, and product lifecycle.

---

### Mission generation

Transform learning into role-specific actions.

The same sustainability topic could therefore produce different missions.

For example:

**Topic: Circular Economy**

Designer:

> Identify one design decision that affects product repairability.

Procurement:

> Identify one supplier criterion related to circularity.

Sales:

> Identify one customer question related to product lifecycle.

---

### Reflection interpretation

Understand what the employee discovered and suggest a logical next step.

---

### Learning explanation

Allow employees to ask questions such as:

> Explain this in simpler terms.

> Give me an example from sales.

> How does this apply to my company?

> What should I remember from this?

---

# 12. Progress and Motivation

Traditional learning platforms often use:

- Course completion
- Percentages
- Certificates
- Points

Learnsy could instead emphasize meaningful progress.

For example:

## Your impact

**8 topics explored**

**6 missions completed**

**4 insights applied at work**

**3 sustainability conversations started**

This makes progress feel connected to behavior rather than content consumption.

Light gamification could still exist, but it should support curiosity rather than become the main motivation.

Possible elements:

- Mission streaks
- Exploration milestones
- Topic badges
- Team challenges

But these should remain secondary.

---

# 13. Optional Social Layer

Employees could optionally share useful discoveries.

Example:

## What your colleagues discovered

**Maria — Procurement**

> Suppliers were much more prepared to discuss emissions data than I expected.

**Jonas — Product**

> We realized repairability isn't currently considered during early product design.

Instead of a leaderboard, this creates a shared organizational learning layer.

Employees can see:

**“People like me are discovering useful things.”**

# 14. Sustainability Manager View

A second side of the concept could support sustainability managers.

Instead of only seeing:

**87% completed the sustainability course**

they could see:

### Organization learning signals

Employees learning about:

- Scope 3
- Circular economy
- ESG reporting
- Green claims

Actions taken:

- 24 customer conversations
- 18 supplier reviews
- 12 process improvements identified

Common employee questions:

- What emissions information should we give customers?
- How do sustainability requirements affect procurement?
- What does this regulation mean for my role?

This could help the sustainability team understand where employees need more guidance.

Importantly, this part would need thoughtful privacy design so employees do not feel that every reflection is being monitored.

# 15. Key Design Principle

The entire concept should follow one rule:

## Never recommend learning without explaining why it matters.

The core loop becomes:

**Why this matters to me**

↓

**One useful thing to learn**

↓

**One realistic action**

↓

**What did I discover?**

↓

**What should I explore next?**

That is the heart of Learnsy Missions.

# 19. Why This Fits the Learnsy Challenge

The challenge asks how learning can become something employees are curious to follow rather than something they simply feel required to complete.

Learnsy Missions approaches that by shifting the experience from:

**Course catalogue**

to:

**Personal journey**

From:

**Mandatory learning**

to:

**Relevant discovery**

From:

**Content completion**

to:

**Workplace action**

And from:

**What course should I take?**

to:

**What could I understand or do next?**

The concept therefore focuses directly on the questions Learnsy asks in its challenge:

- Would employees actually want to use it?
- Would they know what to learn next?
- Would they understand how learning connects to their work?
- Would they remain curious enough to continue?

The intended answer is not created through more content.

It is created through **relevance, action, and adaptive learning**.
